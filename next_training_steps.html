Next training steps (relation extraction, gpu_bundle)

How to get both training and relation extraction onto actual Results sentences. Written 2026-10-01.

1. The problem: trained on abstracts, applied to Results sections

text type

Extraction (step 17)Results sentences from 3,822 full-text PMC papers (experimental_ner/ → sentences/)

The models learn from abstract-style text and are applied to Results-section text. Results sentences are longer, mention figures and experimental conditions, and phrase relations differently from an abstract's summary. Two consequences:

  • The benchmark test scores (BioRED test F1 0.68, PPI test F1 0.72) are measured on abstracts, so they say little about precision on your corpus — it may be better or worse.
  • There is no public relation-extraction corpus built from Results sentences. Training labels have to come from your own corpus.

2. The options

1A gold-standard set from your own Results sentences do first

  • Hand-label 500–1,000 entity pairs drawn from sentences/, with BioRED's label set: activates / inhibits / binds / associated / none.
  • Use it as dev + test. It is the first true measure of how the models perform on your text, and the yardstick for every option below.
  • Pre-select with the current models so the sample is balanced, not mostly “none”.
  • Effort: a few days of annotation.

2LLM-labelled training data from your Results sentences do first

  • An LLM labels several thousand entity pairs from your Results sentences with the same label set; a sample (~200) is hand-checked.
  • Fine-tune BioBERT on BioRED + these labels. The fast, cheap BioBERT pipeline still scores all 126k+ pairs, but has learned Results-section phrasing.
  • Effort: API calls over a few thousand sentences, plus the hand-check.

3Labels from curated databases (distant supervision)

  • Label pairs in your Results sentences from curated interaction databases:
    • SIGNOR — signed activation / inhibition / binding between genes
    • IntAct / STRING physical — binding
    • CTD — chemical–gene, chemical–disease
    • DGIdb — already in gpu_bundle/databases/interactions.tsv
  • Cheap and large, but noisy: a database fact does not mean this sentence states it. Best combined with, or filtered by, option 2.

4Adapt the base model to Results text (no labels needed)

  • Continue BioBERT's pre-training (masked language modelling) on the unlabelled Results sentences of the 3,822 papers before fine-tuning. Domain-adaptive pre-training typically adds a few F1 points in-domain.
  • A simpler partial step: switch the base model to BiomedBERT abstract+fulltext (--base-model microsoft/BiomedNLP-BiomedBERT-base-uncased-abstract-fulltext), pre-trained on full-text PMC.

5Keep the benchmark corpora as a starting point

  • Train on BioRED / BioInfer first, then fine-tune on in-domain data from options 2–3.
  • Select the model (epoch, threshold, calibrator) on your dev set from option 1, not on BioRED's.

3. Recommendation

Options 1 and 2 first. Option 1 shows how good the current models really are on Results sentences. Option 2 is the most direct way to train on them. Options 3–4 are add-ons once 1–2 show where the errors are; option 5 is how they fit together.

4. Already ruled out or done

itemfinding
Truncation at 128 tokens Not a limit. The second entity is cut off for 0.6% of BioRED test positives and 0% of PPI test positives; median rows are 49–77 tokens. Step 17 already scores your corpus at 256 tokens.
PPI dev/test leak Fixed in bigbio_to_re.py: dev is carved by document, not by row (dev rows with a near-copy in train: 95% → 1%). PPI needs retraining with the fix.
Metrics record Each training run now writes summaries/metrics_<task>.json and <model>/metrics.json: test metrics, per-class P/R/F1, confusion matrix, per-epoch dev curve, calibration.
Comparison with published results Scores are in line with the literature: BioRED pair + relation type F1 0.52–0.59 (document level), BioInfer PPI F1 ~0.74 (BioBERT). No published relation-extraction result on these corpora is near 0.90.
